Where the noise comes from

Wording will not fix this. You already have behaviors named be-brief-on-simple-tasks and open-sessions-without-asking, and the day above happened with both switched on. The structure asks for a message per event, so messages come. Seven causes, each with its change.

One message today

  1. A session runs tower queue t7k "From t7h: …".
  2. The tower files it as a task under whatever you asked last.
  3. It delivers the message and starts watching the receiver.
  4. The receiver finishes. The brain gets a [watch] note.
  5. The brain reads the receiver's screen and marks the task verified.
  6. It writes you that the answer “has now gone to” the other session.

The reply starts the same six steps again. This is why it looks as if the tower moved the message.

One message, proposed

  1. A session runs the same command. The tower records who sent it.
  2. It delivers the message when the receiver is free, with the sender's name on it.
  3. The message joins that space's thread on the board.

The brain is not woken and you are told nothing. Two things still surface: a message that cannot be delivered, and a decision that is yours.

This is where the brain's day went: 74 screen reads and 73 task verifications, against 3 instructions sent. A behavior telling it not to relay would change nothing, because it is not relaying. The change belongs in the daemon.

One rule

The tower speaks in two cases: you asked, or the decision is yours. Everything else it writes down.

Yours

A decision only you can make

Interrupts once. The orb shows a count and one card appears. Merges, deploys, deletes, production data, secrets, money, design choices.

Asked

The answer to something you said

One reply, in the place you asked. A command gets a receipt. It never gets a question.

Record

Everything else

Starts, deliveries, finishes, session-to-session messages, routine answers, retries. Written to the board. Never spoken, never pushed.

How much it tells you

One setting. Today quiet comes from focus: you silence the spaces you are not in, and that runs out after eight hours. Here every space is quiet until you follow it, and working in one space is a click in the sidebar.

It speaks when

  • You said something out loud. It answers once.
  • A decision of yours arrives. One sentence, once.
  • You say “catch me up”. Three sentences at most, decisions first.

It never speaks for

  • Deliveries, starts and finishes.
  • One session writing to another.
  • A check that passed, a task verified, a retry that worked.
  • Anything that ends in “nothing for you”.

Per space there are two switches. Follow sends you that space's results as they land. Mute keeps its decisions in its own tower and off the orb. Both are in the mockup above, at the top of each space.

Smaller changes that add up

Make it a rule

After you answer a card, one tap turns the answer into a behavior, such as “on the landing page, take the tower's pick for drawing choices”. Your 10 percent shrinks as you use it.

Merge in order

A repo's pull requests stack in one card, dev before main, with one button. 29 merge prompts in a day become a handful.

Names you can read

A session is its space and its project: “sandbox › mediaroom”. Today the same session can appear as “Claude in eyz mediaroom serverless”.

Colour means state

Amber is yours, cyan is working, grey is quiet, rose failed. Account badges become plain text and show only when the account is not the default.

The record keeps everything

Chat and Activity merge into one searchable log, filtered by space. All 277 of the day's messages would still be there to read.

Build order

The first three steps need no new interface. By the day's own numbers they take 277 messages down to roughly 70.

  1. Silence the note turns

    is_news_prompt already tells a [watch] or [queue] turn from a request. On those turns, write nothing to the chat and speak nothing. Replace “Tell the user what happened” and “Never answer one with silence” with one command the brain uses to raise a decision.

    assets/tower-core.md · src/brain.rs · src/daemon/narrate.rs

  2. Take the tower out of the path between sessions

    tower queue records who called it, from HC_TERMINAL_ID, the way tower screenshot already does. A message from a session is delivered with the same guarantees and kept as a thread. It makes no agenda task, starts no watch, and sends the brain no [queue], [watch] or [agenda] note. The tower adds the sender's name, so sessions stop writing “From t7h” by hand.

    src/main.rs · src/protocol.rs · src/daemon/sendq.rs · src/daemon/watch.rs · src/agenda.rs · src/introduce.rs

  3. Fix what counts as needing you

    A session with no open task that says it is ready is ready. Give each decision one key (the PR, or the normalised question) so repeats and several askers update one card.

    src/questions.rs · src/daemon/asks.rs · src/prs.rs

  4. Widen the window and make the board its home

    The panel is 380 × 640 (panel::W, panel::H). Keep that size for the floating panel, and add a full window of about 960 × 640 with a sidebar: All spaces, then each open space. View has Chat, Activity, Spaces, Phone, Behaviors, Shots and Session; add Board as the default, showing what the sidebar selects. A request typed in a space reaches the brain tagged with that space. Chat and Activity become Record. Order the cards by sessions waiting times minutes waited, and let the number keys answer the first one. This gives you the sidebar and the view per space while one brain still does the work.

    src/orb/panel.rs · src/orb/mod.rs · src/orb/panel/questions.rs · src/orb/panel/prchip.rs

  5. Let spaces fold

    A space with no live session folds into the quiet line and archives after a day. Its needs close when its sessions are gone.

    src/spaces.rs

  6. Give the orb its count and the floating panel

    The red dot becomes an amber number of open decisions. Hovering the orb peeks at the floating panel and a click pins it. The floating panel is the queue column of the full window, reused, so both stay in step.

    src/orb/mod.rs · src/orb/render.rs · src/orb/panel.rs

  7. Give each open space its own tower

    Start one brain per open space, such as tower-sandbox, holding only that space's sessions, agenda and thread. The daemon already knows each session's space, so notes and requests route by space, and a space's messages can no longer land under another space's request. The main tower keeps what crosses spaces. A space tower starts with the space's first session and stops with its last. Each one is another Claude session running, so do this after steps 1 to 3 have cut the load.

    src/brain.rs · src/daemon/mod.rs · src/spaces.rs · src/agenda.rs · assets/tower-core.md

  8. Make a space a mission

    tower space agenda already stores a goal per space, and no space has one. Ask for the goal when a space is made, add what counts as done, and let the space's tower close it with a report and archive it. A standing space is marked as one and never closes.

    src/spaces.rs · assets/behaviors/spaces-and-focus.md

  9. Launch a space from one sentence

    A saved setup names the repos and the account. tower dispatch already takes a plan with a task per project, so a launch is a setup plus your goal, with no questions back.

    src/dispatch.rs · src/config.rs · src/orb/panel.rs

  10. Give every space its own worktrees

    isolate already puts one task's session in a git worktree. Make that the default for every session a space opens, give each a dev port of its own, and remove both when the space closes.

    src/dispatch.rs · src/main.rs

  11. Bring the phone in line

    The phone API already serves questions, pull requests, spaces, screenshots and voice. Add the ordered queue, each space's goal and state, launch and close, and Follow and Mute. In the app, make the queue the first screen and notify for decisions only.

    docs/mobile-api.md · src/daemon/lan/mod.rs · the herdr-tower-android repo
